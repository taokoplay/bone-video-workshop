# Dramatic craft, manju adaptation and review rubric

Contents: [Applicability](#applicability), [Scenes](#scene-level-check),
[Dialogue](#dialogue-and-performance), [Continuity](#blocking-and-continuity),
[Manju](#manju-and-ai-production), [Findings](#finding-and-repair-contract).

## Applicability

Read this for episode writing, professional review or a scoped revision. Apply
criteria to the actual brief, genre, running time and intended presentation.
Do not invent fixed episode counts, scene counts, word quotas, mandatory paywalls,
romance, revenge, moral alignment or a cliffhanger for every kind of work.
Source skill popularity does not validate a storytelling rule. User constraints
and established canon remain above external templates and reviewer taste.

For adaptation, identify original facts versus approved departures. For original
work, identify established versus proposed decisions. Missing facts may be left
open in a proposal; they cannot silently become accepted history. A character
profile is not evidence that the screenplay dramatizes that character's trait.

## Scene-level check

For each source scene, use a compact diagnostic row in findings/planning:

`scene | entry state | focal goal | resistance | tactic/action | response |
result/change | exit state | evidence | effect on next scene`

Read the entire scene, not only logline/beat synopsis. Ask:

- Why does the action happen now? What causes the next decision?
- Does the opposition act from its own supported motive rather than stop acting
  merely to let the protagonist win? Minor functional characters need not all
  receive secret agendas or full arcs.
- What actually changes: information, risk, power, relationship, emotion or a
  physical situation? Quiet geography/transition scenes can be purposeful;
  do not delete them just because they lack a dramatic reversal.
- Can the important change be seen/heard through action, response or deliberate
  speech? "Realizes the truth" needs a source, realization and consequence.
- Does a twist reinterpret planted evidence, or introduce a convenient new fact?
- Does the audience receive what this episode promised, or an intentional,
  credible deferral? A cliffhanger does not replace local progression/payoff.

Use flaws with evidence as revision grounds. "More cinematic" alone is not a
finding. Do not require every sentence to contain conflict, every scene to turn
polarity or every character secret to be revealed in episode one.

## Dialogue and performance

Extract an optional line sheet from the complete prose without replacing it:
`scene | speaker | exact spoken text | intent/subtext | delivery | onscreen/VO/OS |
listener response | estimated/actual duration | timing assumption`.

Check whether each line pursues, avoids, tests, threatens, comforts, decides or
changes the relationship. Preserve deliberate pauses and authorial idiosyncrasy;
not all language must be converted to the same clipped colloquial voice.
Compare vocabulary, forms of address and what each character knows. If swapping
speaker names changes nothing, inspect the scene for generic dialogue rather
than automatically adding catchphrases. Dialogue and VO are choices with duties,
not a universal hierarchy; don't add narration to cover a missing visual event.

Count spoken words/characters separately from speaker labels, punctuation,
stage directions and on-screen text. State language/pace assumptions. Actions,
reactions and speech can overlap; sum the critical path, not every category.
Estimates are not recorded performances. No fixed four-characters-per-second
rule proves fit; silence, breath, numbers and pronunciation need actual delivery.
Do not shorten exact user lines or alter their intention outside permission.

## Blocking and continuity

Before specifying a camera, establish where characters and objects physically
are, who is present at scene entry, who arrives later, and what supports whom.
Use world coordinates/location landmarks first, then derive screen direction
from actual camera placement. A prompt-based layout remains a hypothesis until
visual references confirm it; don't claim an unseen platform or doorway exists.

For each adjacent pair inspect:

`A selected exit -> visible transition/ellipsis -> B selected entry`

Compare action phase, facing, eyeline, prop owner/hand, clothing/injury, geography,
light contribution, time, knowledge, emotional state and sound. Use the previous-
current-next window when fixing one shot. Classify: continuous on text; explained
change; concrete repair required; or not verifiable without missing evidence.
These are report descriptions, not unsupported new plugin verdict enums.

A cut can omit preparation or part of a walk when the result is understandable.
Do not demand real-time coverage of every step. Preserve indispensable contact,
transfer of support, causal evidence and information-order changes. The planned
exitState cannot prove an event happened when no shot actually depicts or
legitimately implies it. Camera movement never automatically moves the actors.

## Manju and AI production

Identify the actual production form: static comic panels with voice/camera moves,
limited character animation, fully animated scenes, photoreal AI drama or mixed
production. Do not force photoreal action into every comic work, or call a still
pan performed character animation. Clarify only if the choice materially changes
the requested result and cannot be inferred.

- Preserve stable character silhouette, costume, identifying marks and relative
  scale; keep scene-specific poses/states out of identity definitions.
- Reference actual accepted images by real IDs; model negative prompts do not
  guarantee stable hands, mirrored markings or lip sync.
- Use reusable art where appropriate, but don't let reuse erase state changes,
  injuries, possessions or newly learned facts.
- Pick the necessary camera angle and action unit. An intentional continuous
  shot can change framing; hidden cuts and incompatible locations need separate
  coverage or an explicit long-take plan, not rigid one-size rules.
- For group action, props/contact and reflections, plan phases and clean layers,
  then use a technical sample before expensive full production. Describe a
  fallback and observable trigger for using it; don't claim every model always
  succeeds or always fails at a class of action.
- Separate performance instructions, frame artwork prompts, video motion,
  spoken text, sound design and graphic overlays. Full script pasted into every
  generator is not a production prompt.
- Preserve edit handles and multiple viable cut points. Do not request unnatural
  accelerated performance merely to make the raw file match a two-second edit.
- Measure actual footage before selecting in/out. Explain why each selection
  starts and ends at that action/response; invalid regions are not usable handles.

## Finding and repair contract

Use one structured finding per distinct cause, inside record_review.findings or
an expanded companion report:

`finding label | source/field/location | evidence | severity | impact |
responsible layer | preserve | smallest repair | acceptance criterion |
affected neighbors/dependencies | remaining uncertainty`

Severity is a judgment with a stated consequence, not a star grade. Separate
hard-constraint breaches, production risks and preferences. A number of fields,
a high score, a tool success or several simulated agreeing reviewers cannot
prove artistic quality. Mark findings closed only after rereading the changed
content and checking the same criterion; mark actual-media conclusions
not-verified until observed. Do not claim that textual continuity establishes
a real cut's fluidity or that measured duration means dialogue was listened to.

For review-driven changes, follow revision-workflow.md: scope the operation,
stage the proposal, apply only under the user's authorization, and recheck the
source and impacted neighbors. Improving the plugin itself never authorizes
editing a production used as its example.
